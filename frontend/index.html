How the numbers work

Premium written
Annual premium of policies that start in the period. This is what sales see.
Premium earned
The part of each annual premium that covers days inside the period (premium × days covered ÷ 365). A policy sold last month has only earned a month. This is the fair yardstick for claims.
Claims paid
Money paid on claims whose loss happened in the period, after the deductible.
Open claims
Claims not settled yet, set aside at the claim amount minus the deductible. Prudent: historically about 95% of that is paid.
Loss ratio
Claims (paid plus open) ÷ premium earned.
Commission and costs
Assumptions, not in the data: broker commission on premium sold by brokers, and operating costs on all premium. Change them under Filters.
Result after costs
Premium earned minus claims, commission and operating costs. Shown in parentheses and red ink when negative.
Combined ratio
(Claims + commission + costs) ÷ premium earned. Above 100% means losing money.
Chance of bad luck
How likely a loss this size is from normal randomness alone, using the book's own claim sizes over the last year (a compound Poisson model). The check only flags losses where this is under 5%.

What the data can't tell us

  • Operating costs and commission rates aren't in the extract, so results after costs depend on the assumptions.
  • Cancelled policies have no cancellation date, so they're counted as covered for the full year (2.9% of policies).
  • Renewal terms aren't separate rows: every claim in the data falls in a policy's first year, so only first-year premium is counted.
  • Claims for the most recent months are still coming in, so the latest quarter can look better than it will end up.
  • A cluster of claims under $10,000 is a reason to review, not proof of fraud.
